Sun tone curve

The owner reported the sun looking “as if a sheet of frosted glass were laid over it — not fiery red like it used to be”. Until 3f09796 the universe, forest and fallback families rendered with no tone curve at all: EffectComposer sets gl.toneMapping = NoToneMapping on mount and none of those three chains contained a <ToneMapping> pass, so everything above 1.0 in linear space hit the display ceiling flat and stayed fully saturated. Restoring a curve was right. The curve restored was AgX, and AgX desaturates as it approaches the top of its range — that is how it avoids the clipping. The report describes AgX working as designed.

Four curves, applied to the colours the two reported worlds' suns actually hand the renderer. Every number here is produced by toneCurves.mjs, the same module measure.mjs checks.

Saturation kept, against the build the report compares against

Averaged over the texture ramp, both worlds, star surface and glow shell. 1.00 is the old build's look.

What this does not show. A frame is not these swatches. Bloom, the grade, the vignette, the film grain and the glow shell's additive blend over the star all sit between this colour and a rendered pixel, and none of them is modelled here. What makes the comparison fair anyway is that every one of them is identical across the four columns — the tone curve is the only thing that differs — so the difference between columns is the difference in the frame, even though the absolute values are not. It also does not model the sun texture's own hue, only its brightness ramp.
